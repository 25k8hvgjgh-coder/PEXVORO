# ReconFeed Music Licensing Plan

**Status:** Planning draft — not legal advice; no music rights are represented as cleared.

## Product decision
ReconFeed will use a built-in, curated music picker for user-generated short videos. Ads and paid memberships may be introduced later. Do not expose commercial tracks until the rights for the intended use and launch territory have been documented and approved.

## Recommended launch path
1. Start with a small catalog of independent artists and production-music providers willing to grant explicit written rights for user-generated audiovisual videos on a social platform.
2. Require clearance for both (a) the musical composition and (b) the specific sound recording/master, unless the agreement clearly establishes that one party controls both.
3. Verify that the grant covers synchronization with video, necessary reproduction/caching, upload, hosting, streaming/public performance where applicable, user-generated content, clip lengths, in-app discovery/previews, social sharing, promotional use, ads, and subscriptions/memberships.
4. Limit tracks by territory, date, allowed use, and any clip-duration rules. Use only catalog assets supplied or authorized by the licensor.
5. Keep a rights ledger and usage reports. Support fast disabling/removal of a track and identify videos that use it.
6. Before monetization or a mainstream catalog launch, have a U.S. music/entertainment attorney review the rights matrix and contracts.

## Rights checklist per track
- Track ID, title, artist, version, ISRC if supplied, composition identifiers if supplied.
- Composition owner(s), publisher(s), recording/master owner, licensing contact.
- Written agreement and evidence of authority to grant each right.
- Approved territories and effective/expiration dates.
- Approved formats and uses: UGC synchronization, reproduction, hosting, streaming, previews, promotional use, social sharing, ads, subscriptions/memberships.
- Clip duration and any editing, looping, remix, lyrics, or attribution restrictions.
- Fees/royalty basis, reporting cadence, audit rights, payment terms.
- Takedown procedure, wind-down terms, treatment of already-published videos after termination.
- Approval status: draft, pending_review, approved, restricted, expired, blocked.
- Reviewer, approval date, contract reference, next review date.

## Provider outreach brief
Describe ReconFeed as an interactive short-form video platform where users select a track from a built-in library and synchronize it with user-generated videos. Explain that ads and memberships are planned but not necessarily live. Ask the provider to specify rights, catalog, territory, restrictions, fees/minimum guarantees, reporting, takedowns, and launch requirements in writing.

Contacting a performing-rights organization may help with public-performance rights, but do not assume that such a license also clears synchronization or master-recording rights. Ask every prospective provider to identify rights included and excluded in its quote.

## Release gates
- [ ] Written counsel-reviewed rights matrix approved.
- [ ] Each visible track is approved for the viewer's territory and the actual product use.
- [ ] Music is not selectable when its rights are missing, expired, disputed, or restricted.
- [ ] Track ID is stored with each post and usage events are reportable.
- [ ] Admin can disable a track and locate affected posts quickly.
- [ ] Copyright complaint and repeat-infringer procedures are published and operational.
- [ ] Privacy policy, Terms of Service, and community rules reviewed for UGC music.
- [ ] Monetization changes reviewed against existing agreements before ads/memberships launch.

## Guardrails
- Do not label a song licensed based only on a user saying they have permission.
- Do not assume short duration, attribution, noncommercial use, or a consumer music subscription makes the use lawful.
- Do not scrape or stream music from consumer services.
- Do not make a track available based on an unsigned quote or verbal assurance.
- Do not promise users a permanent right to keep videos using a track after the license ends unless the agreement allows it.
